# Gate first, then screen cheap cross-field fixes

The best cheap bets for moving calibIllit's 74.7% (subject-specific ShallowConvNet on per-session Riemannian-aligned input, BCI IV-2a T→E) come from two places. The first is machine-learning **augmentation priors**, which already have published gains on the identical within-subject session-1→session-2 protocol. The second is **test-time entropy/information-maximisation with label smoothing**, borrowed from vision TTA and speech unsupervised adaptation, worth +1.45pp on this protocol. Both are drop-in with the now-importable braindecode 1.7.0 or about 40 lines of PyTorch. Next come two cheap, uncertain bets: near-free regularisation (leave-one-run-out epoch selection, SWA) and a group of 1-2 hour linear-pipeline screens borrowed from speech, chemometrics and semi-supervised learning (per-session score standardisation, MAP class-mean adaptation, pseudo-label self-training). Most of the "geometric" imports should not be run blind. These are chemometric calibration transfer, blind equalisation, Wasserstein-Procrustes and pseudo-label Riemannian rotation. Published within-subject numbers on IV-2a show unsupervised rotation *losing* to no adaptation (59.5% vs 69.8%), and the project has already closed ICP, CPD and learned geometric correction, which are close relatives. These methods should be gated behind an **oracle-rotation test that uses the true eval labels as an upper bound**. Several more families can be dropped outright on published evidence: Tent, T3A, CoTTA, DANN, nonlinear OT, TCA, plain OT-DA, DS/PDS, and responder prediction at n=9. Because n=9 can only resolve effects of about 3pp, every screen below is exploratory with a pre-declared kill threshold. Only a handful of survivors, or a stack of small survivors, should go to one pre-registered confirmatory run, ideally replicated on a second multi-session MOABB dataset to escape the n=9 ceiling.

## Four diagnostics gate whole method families before anything is built

The single most valuable hour is spent finding out whether a family has any headroom. The project's own evidence frames the question. Per-session whitening helped (+4.7pp linear, +11.4pp convnet). Per-layer BN on top of it did nothing (+0.08pp). So the residual day-shift is not a per-channel or per-band gain, and three candidates remain: an orthogonal rotation left by whitening, class-conditional shifts, and within-session drift. Each candidate has a cheap test that uses eval labels **only to compute an upper bound, never to fit a reported method**.

**D1, the oracle-rotation test (about 1 h).** This gates every rotation/congruence import, i.e. pseudo-label RPA/TSA, speech EM-CMLLR, Wasserstein Procrustes, unsupervised-anchor TSA and congruence-constrained Procrustes. The procedure is: re-centre both sessions, then fit `pyriemann.transfer.TLRotate` with the **true** eval labels, then measure the gain over re-centering alone. Run it on CSP+LDA, MDM and TS+LR. In pyriemann 0.12, TLRotate matches per-class means and rotates *source* domains onto the target. It raises an error unless target labels are present, which local source inspection confirmed ([pyriemann API](https://pyriemann.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api.html)). Because of that orientation, make the labelled train session the target and the eval session the "source", so the trained classifier stays fixed. If even the oracle buys less than 2pp mean, no label-free estimate of the rotation can do better, and the whole family is closed in an hour. The prior that the oracle gain is small is real. Kobler et al. report unsupervised RPA at **59.5%** on BNCI2014001 inter-session, against **69.8%** for non-adapted FB+TSM+LR and **71.3%** for simple domain-specific standardisation ([Kobler et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01323)). Bleuzé et al. describe the unlabelled-anchor version of their tangent-space Procrustes as possibly "totally inaccurate" ([Bleuzé et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2022.1049985)).

**D2, the oracle class-mean test (about 1 h, same script).** Keep the T-session LDA/MDM covariance and replace only the class means with true E-session class means. Do the same with only the pooled mean (the PMean bias). The gap between these two numbers bounds what any unsupervised **class-conditional** correction can buy. That covers speech MAP adaptation, adaptive MDM, T3A-style prototypes and pseudo-label ComBat. If the class-mean oracle beats the pooled-mean oracle by under 2pp, those methods are closed.

**D3, the per-run drift diagnostic (about 10 min).** Split eval-session predictions by run (6 runs × 48 trials) and test for a monotone accuracy trend, and for a trend in the distance of each run's mean covariance from the session mean. A flat profile kills the online-tracking family: Vidaurre pooled-mean tracking, recursive Riemannian re-centering, Kalman-style tracking, `exponential_moving_standardize`, and OSPDIM. The published prior already points to a small effect. In Wimpff et al.'s IV-2a cross-session experiments, online and batch RA differ by about 0.1pp (76.77% vs 76.91%) ([Wimpff et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18520v2)). Arvaneh et al.'s continuous re-estimation beat single estimation by only 73.1% vs 72.1% (p=0.067) ([Arvaneh et al. 2013](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/ctguan/Publications/2013_Mahnaz_Neural_Comp.pdf)).

**D4, the pseudo-label reliability and spectral-shift diagnostic (about 1 h).** For each subject, sort eval predictions by confidence and report the accuracy of the top 25/50/75%. This is calibration, not a method. If the confident half of weak subjects 2/5/6 is not substantially above their 57-60% overall, every self-training, label-propagation, pseudo-label rotation and distillation method will amplify errors exactly where help is needed. Confirmation bias is the dominant documented failure of EEG self-training ([PMC6925734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6925734)). In the same hour, fit the mu/beta peak frequency on each session's unlabelled spectrum. If |Δpeak| between T and E is under about 0.5 Hz for every subject, VTLN-style warping has nothing to correct. A cheap psychometric add-on is a class-confusion breakdown per subject, possibly as a 10-line Rasch-style logistic GLM separating subject ability from class difficulty. It shows whether weak subjects fail on one class pair. Bauer & Gharabaghi's IRT framing of BCI ability is the nearest precedent ([PMC4329795](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4329795)). This analysis is diagnostic only and should not be expected to raise accuracy.

### Why this order and not another

**Augmentation tops the list because it is the only import with published gains on exactly this protocol**, and because braindecode 1.7.0's transforms make it one constructor call per arm. Rommel et al. evaluated 13 transforms on 2a within-subject session-1→session-2. They found FTSurrogate best overall, TimeReverse best at low data, and SmoothTimeMask and ChannelsDropout best on larger sets. SignFlip is a no-op for ShallowConvNet because of the squaring layer, and ChannelsShuffle and ChannelsSymmetry hurt ([Rommel et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14483v2)). Hyperparameters should be fixed a priori from that paper (FTSurrogate phase noise 0.9, FrequencyShift ±2.7 Hz, SmoothTimeMask 1.6 s), never tuned on E. Two caveats temper expectations. Rommel's gains are reported mostly at reduced training fractions, so the full-288-trial gain will be smaller. Their network is described as a CSP-inspired braindecode convnet but is not confirmed to be ShallowConvNet. FrequencyShift is the cross-field bridge here: it is the training-time version of speech VTLN, so it competes directly with row 12. Run it first. If it absorbs subject 5's 7.3 Hz peak problem, the hand-built warp is redundant.

**TTA ranks third, not first, because its known gain is small and overlaps what the project already has.** Wimpff's +1.45pp exists only with heavy label smoothing. The project's per-session BN already captures their BN-1 step (+2.1pp for them, ≈0 here), and the 5-seed ensemble at 78.3% matches their best non-EM configuration ([Wimpff et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18520v2)). Ensembles also smooth outputs, so TTA must be screened on the ensemble as well as the single model. Speech's LHUC is a natural extension: one learned gain per hidden unit, r = 2σ(θ), trained on E with pseudo-label cross-entropy. Test-only LHUC came within about 0.1% WER of SAT-LHUC in speech ([Swietojanski et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.02828)). Add it only if row 3 survives, and close it if it merely equals BN-affine entropy minimisation.

**The linear-pipeline rows (4, 6, 7, 9, 11) are cheap but aim at the wrong target.** They lift CSP+LDA from 66.9% and do not touch the 74.7% convnet. Their value is twofold: they are minutes-to-hours probes of whether class-conditional shift or pseudo-label quality is exploitable at all, and their survivors can be ported to convnet features. Row 4 also corrects a likely blind spot. In the only table that compares them on IV-2a inter-session, plain domain-specific standardisation beat every Riemannian and OT alignment method ([Kobler et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01323)). Apicella et al. likewise found that normalisation choice alone rivals domain adaptation on 2a, at 67.82% cross-session ([Apicella et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01081)).

**The genuinely untried imports (rows 12-17) sit low because novelty is not expected value.** Each targets a mechanism the project's own nulls already make unlikely. AJD gain equalisation and CovBat assume a gain- or moment-type residual, and AdaBN and ComBat already found none. RUV is the strongest of them conceptually. It removes *specific directions* of session variation, rotation-like ones included, using controls that cost no labels, and IV-2a's 2 s fixation period supplies those controls for free. Run it only after D1 shows the rotation residual is real.

## Drop these leads before spending an hour on them

Several leads are already ruled out by published within-protocol numbers or by the project's own closures. They should be recorded as "not screened, with reason" rather than silently skipped, because that record is part of avoiding p-hacking.

The **vision TTA family beyond entropy minimisation** has published negatives on IV-2a. In the T-TIME benchmark on BNCI2014001, Tent (74.32%) ties BN-adapt (74.54%), T3A loses about 6pp (68.23%) and CoTTA loses about 2.5pp (72.04%) ([T-TIME](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07228v1)). Since AdaBN is already null here (+0.08pp), Tent-style BN-affine updates without smoothing have essentially no prior. **Adversarial and nonlinear-OT domain adaptation** also fails on this data. On the SKADA-Bench BCI tangent task, mapping OT and JDOT fell to 0.47 and TCA/TSL to 0.26 against a no-DA 0.55, and deep DANN reached 0.41 ([SKADA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11676)). Kobler et al. found ShallowConvNet+DANN at 51.6% vs 51.3% without it ([Kobler et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01323)). **Plain OT-DA (BOTDA/FOTDA)** did not beat Euclidean Alignment in block-wise cross-session MI: 71.5% vs 73.6%, and 77.6% vs 79.9% ([Peterson et al. 2022](https://sinc.unl.edu.ar/sinc-publications/2022/PNWGLMS22/sinc_PNWGLMS22.pdf)). SPD-manifold OT reached 66.8%, under non-adapted FB+TSM+LR on IV-2a ([Kobler et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01323)). For the convnet, **switching RA for EA** is also out: EA hurt cross-session in Wimpff's runs (75.43% vs source 76.16%), while RA helped ([Wimpff et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18520v2)).

The **point-set-registration route to the rotation** is effectively closed by the project's own results. Wasserstein Procrustes (Sinkhorn coupling alternating with orthogonal Procrustes) and unsupervised-anchor TSA are soft-correspondence versions of ICP and CPD, which this project already closed, alongside learned geometric correction. The recently announced SPDAlign uses exactly this Wasserstein-Procrustes idea, but only its abstract was seen ([arXiv 2610.06315](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.06315)). Unless D1 shows an oracle rotation gain well above 2pp, this route, the 1-day congruence-constrained version, and per-session ICA with Hungarian component matching should not be built. ICA matching also carries catastrophic sign and permutation failure modes on 22-channel data.

Several leads are inapplicable by construction. **DS/PDS calibration transfer** needs paired transfer standards measured on both instruments, and EEG sessions have none. **GCT-PLS** needs paired samples, and **MDWM** and labelled **TLRotate** need eval labels ([pyriemann API](https://pyriemann.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api.html)). Standard-free chemometric transfer such as CTAI reduces to affine standardisation that re-centering already performs ([Zhao et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6539942/)). Any method whose estimator pools people violates the no-cross-subject rule: **x-vector conditioning, cross-subject SAT, longitudinal ComBat, ssCSP, multi-source OT built on other subjects, and Azab-style cross-subject active learning**. These are also the cross-field twins of the project's closed population shrinkage, eigenvoice and meta-episode leads. **Plain ComBat and neuroHarmonize** collapse to per-feature location/scale when the class covariate is unknown on E, and the project has already closed that. **Active learning** is artificial offline, because cued trials have fixed labels.

**Responder prediction** is unscreenable at this sample size. The canonical SMR predictor explains about 28% of variance (r=0.53, N=80) ([Blankertz et al. 2010](https://pure.korea.ac.kr/en/publications/neurophysiological-predictor-of-smr-based-bci-performance/)), and a later study of 33 novices did not replicate a significant neurophysiological predictor ([Frontiers 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00574/pdf)). With nine subjects and three weak ones, the confidence interval on any correlation spans nearly the whole range, so compute it descriptively at most. Finally, within the augmentation family, **SignFlip, ChannelsShuffle, ChannelsSymmetry and GaussianNoise** are pre-excluded on Rommel's results. **Cropped training** showed no significant benefit for ShallowConvNet in Schirrmeister's analysis ([Schirrmeister thesis](https://robintibor.github.io/eeg-deep-learning-phd-thesis/MovementDecoding.html)). **ADAPT** forces a TensorFlow install for shallow methods that skada provides without it.

## Screening protocol: many cheap looks, one honest test

The arithmetic behind the protocol is unforgiving. At 288 eval trials and about 75% accuracy, one subject's accuracy carries a binomial standard error near 2.6pp. With nine subjects and a between-subject spread in Δ of a few points, a paired test at 80% power resolves only effects of roughly 3pp (d ≈ 1). An exact sign test needs **8 of 9** subjects improving to reach two-sided p<0.05 (p=0.039); 7 of 9 gives p=0.18. These figures are my calculations, not sourced. Screening fifteen-odd methods at a +2pp promotion bar makes a false survivor likely (roughly a one-in-three chance by my estimate when seed-to-seed noise is about 1pp). A screen result is therefore never evidence. It is a ticket into the confirmatory stage.

**Stage 1 is exploratory and logged before it runs.** For every screen, write the method, its fixed hyperparameters (taken from the source paper, never tuned on E labels), the comparator, the kill threshold from the table above, and the promotion bar into a screening log, and commit it before execution. The project already commits pre-registrations, so the log can live beside them. Run alignment-type screens on the fast linear pipelines (CSP+LDA, MDM, TS+LR on RA input) and port only survivors to ShallowConvNet. Run augmentation and TTA screens directly on the convnet with three seeds. Report every screen, killed or not, with mean Δ, the count of non-negative subjects, the worst-subject Δ, and a separate Δ for weak subjects 2/5/6. Where a method needs a hyperparameter, pick it without E labels. Use leave-one-run-out CV on session T, or an unsupervised scorer such as skada's `CircularValidation`, which correlated best with target accuracy in SKADA-Bench (ρ=0.71) ([SKADA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11676)). The default bars are: **kill below +1pp mean, or if three or more subjects lose more than 2pp; promote at ≥ +2pp mean with ≥7/9 non-negative and no subject below −5pp**. The oracle diagnostics D1-D2 are the only place eval labels touch anything before final scoring, and they gate families. They never select a specific configuration.

**Small survivors are stacked, not tested one by one.** Rows 4, 5, 9 and 11 can each be real yet sit below detectability at about 1pp. The honest move is to pre-declare one "stack" hypothesis combining every cheap survivor and confirm the stack as a single test, rather than running several underpowered confirmations.

**Stage 2 is confirmatory and pre-registered.** At most three hypotheses go forward, Holm-corrected. Each runs on a frozen code tag with fresh seeds never used in screening, against the same-seed baseline pipeline (single model and 5-seed ensemble both). The primary endpoint is mean accuracy Δ over the nine subjects, tested by an exact sign-flip permutation test over all 512 sign patterns. The secondary endpoints are the sign test and weak-subject Δ. The cleanest way past the n=9 ceiling is an external within-subject replication on a second multi-session dataset already reachable through the installed moabb 1.5.0. Two candidates: BNCI2015001 was used for cross-session OT and EA comparisons ([Peterson et al. 2022](https://sinc.unl.edu.ar/sinc-publications/2022/PNWGLMS22/sinc_PNWGLMS22.pdf)), and IV-2b was used for cross-session TTA ([Wimpff et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18520v2)). Fix the replication dataset in the pre-registration so it cannot be chosen after seeing results.

## Conclusion

The cross-field sweep changes where the remaining headroom is likely to be. The literature most obviously analogous to "an unknown transform between two days" is chemometric calibration transfer, blind equalisation and point-set alignment. That literature has either already been absorbed by per-session re-centering, or has failed in this exact protocol, or duplicates registration methods this project closed. What remains promising comes from fields that treat the problem as **learning under scarce, noisy data**: augmentation priors that encode spectral invariances, flat-minimum and ensemble regularisation, and smoothed entropy-based test-time adaptation. That is a substantive shift. It suggests the 74.7% ceiling is now limited more by variance from 288 training trials than by an unmodelled geometric session shift. That hypothesis is falsifiable within a day, by D1 together with the augmentation screen.

The second takeaway concerns method, not methods. Three hours of oracle and drift diagnostics can retire more of this list than a week of implementation. Stacking small survivors into one confirmatory hypothesis, then replicating on a second MOABB dataset, is the only route by which effects of 1-2pp, the size most published adaptation gains actually have, can ever be shown with nine subjects.
